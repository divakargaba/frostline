# Cost of Hydrate Events

## Production impact

Lost production from hydrate events can reach **up to 5% of output** (Case 9 brief, IEEE YP Industry Hackathon 2026). For a deepwater well producing 10,000 bbl/day at $80/bbl, that translates to $40,000/day in lost revenue — potentially millions of dollars per event if a full plug requires weeks to remediate.

## Direct costs

- **Chemical inhibitors:** Methanol costs $300-500/tonne; a single intervention may require 10-50 tonnes. MEG is more expensive per unit but recoverable.
- **Intervention operations:** Subsea intervention vessels cost $200,000-500,000/day. Coiled tubing operations add further costs.
- **Equipment damage:** Plug projectile events (from one-sided depressurization) can rupture lines and damage subsea trees, with repair costs in the millions.
- **Deferred production:** The dominant cost. Days to weeks of lost production while a plug is remediated.

## Safety costs

Beyond economics, hydrate plugs pose a direct safety hazard to personnel. The pressure differential across a plug can reach hundreds of bar. If the plug suddenly releases, the resulting projectile effect has caused fatalities in the industry.

## The case for early warning

A system that detects forming hydrates (transient phase) hours before a full plug develops can:
- Enable preemptive inhibitor injection (low cost, quick).
- Avoid the need for costly remediation.
- Prevent safety incidents.
- Maintain production continuity.

The economic value of early warning is the difference between the cost of preemptive treatment (minutes, small chemical volume) and the cost of full remediation (days to weeks of downtime, intervention vessels, potential equipment damage).
