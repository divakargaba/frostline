# Low-Dosage Hydrate Inhibitors (LDHI)

Low-dosage hydrate inhibitors are an alternative to traditional thermodynamic inhibitors (methanol, MEG). They work at much lower concentrations (0.1-3 wt% vs 10-50 wt%) and through different mechanisms.

## Kinetic hydrate inhibitors (KHI)

- **Mechanism:** Delay hydrate nucleation and crystal growth. They do not prevent hydrate formation indefinitely — they buy time (typically 12-72 hours depending on subcooling).
- **Typical dose:** 0.5-3 wt% of active polymer in the water phase.
- **Subcooling limit:** Most KHIs are effective only up to 8-10 C of subcooling. Beyond this, they may fail.
- **Advantages:** Much lower volumes and cost than THIs, no recovery infrastructure needed.
- **Disadvantages:** Time-limited protection, subcooling limit, may not work in high-water-cut systems.
- **Use case:** Short tiebacks, moderate subcooling, transient protection during shutdowns.

## Anti-agglomerants (AA)

- **Mechanism:** Allow hydrate crystals to form but prevent them from agglomerating into a plug. Crystals remain as a transportable slurry in the hydrocarbon phase.
- **Typical dose:** 0.5-2 wt% relative to the water phase.
- **Advantages:** No subcooling limit (work even at high subcooling), continuous protection as long as oil is the continuous phase.
- **Disadvantages:** Require an oil-continuous system (water cut typically < 50-60%), may cause emulsion problems, environmental concerns with some formulations.
- **Use case:** Deepwater systems with moderate water cut where THI logistics are impractical.

## Operational considerations

LDHIs are not a drop-in replacement for THIs in all situations. Selection depends on subcooling severity, water cut, shut-in duration, and field economics. They are often used in combination: e.g., KHI for normal operations plus methanol for planned shutdowns.
