# Hydrate Plug Remediation

Once a hydrate plug has formed, removing it is a slow and hazardous operation. Prevention and early detection are always preferable.

## Depressurization

The most common remediation method. By reducing pressure on one or both sides of the plug, the hydrate equilibrium shifts and the plug slowly dissociates.

- **Two-sided depressurization** (preferred): Reduce pressure on both sides simultaneously. This is safer because the plug melts from both ends, preventing it from becoming a projectile.
- **One-sided depressurization** (hazardous): Reducing pressure on only one side creates a large pressure differential across the plug. If the plug suddenly releases, it can accelerate through the pipe at high velocity — a severe safety risk that has caused fatalities. **One-sided depressurization should be avoided whenever possible.**
- **Timeline:** Dissociation can take hours to weeks depending on plug length, subcooling, and line geometry.
- **Challenges:** In subsea systems, depressurizing both sides may not be feasible without special intervention equipment.

## Heating

Raising the temperature above the hydrate equilibrium point dissociates the plug.

- **Methods:** Electrical heating cables, hot fluid circulation, or heating coils.
- **Advantages:** Can be faster than depressurization alone.
- **Disadvantages:** Difficult and expensive in subsea environments. Heating a long subsea line is often impractical. Localized heating can create trapped high-pressure gas pockets.

## Inhibitor injection

Injecting methanol or MEG into the plug region shifts the equilibrium and promotes dissociation.

- **Method:** Inject through dedicated chemical injection lines or coiled tubing.
- **Advantages:** Can work in conjunction with depressurization.
- **Disadvantages:** Requires access to the plug location, large volumes may be needed, and the inhibitor must reach the hydrate surface.

## Key safety principles

1. Never depressurize from one side only unless no alternative exists and proper safety measures are in place.
2. Do not apply heat to a confined section — trapped gas expansion can cause pipe rupture.
3. Monitor pressure and temperature continuously during remediation.
4. Remediation is slow; plan for extended downtime.
