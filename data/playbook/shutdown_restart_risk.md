# Shutdown and Restart Risk

Planned and unplanned shutdowns are among the highest-risk periods for hydrate formation. During normal production, the flowing fluid carries heat that keeps the line above hydrate conditions. When flow stops, the fluid cools toward the seabed temperature.

## Cooldown time

Cooldown time (or no-touch time) is the time from shutdown until the fluid temperature drops below the hydrate equilibrium temperature at the current trapped pressure.

- **Typical values:** 6-24 hours for insulated deepwater flowlines, as little as 1-2 hours for bare or poorly insulated lines.
- **Critical parameter:** Operations must either restart or inject inhibitors before the cooldown time expires.
- **Factors affecting cooldown:** Insulation quality, pipe diameter, seabed temperature, fluid composition, and trapped pressure.

## Restart risks

Restarting after a cooldown period has expired without inhibitor injection is hazardous:

- Hydrate crystals may have already formed but not yet agglomerated.
- Resuming flow can cause rapid agglomeration and plug formation.
- Pressure surges during restart can push the system further into the hydrate zone.

## Mitigation procedures

1. **Before shutdown:** If planned, consider displacing the flowline with treated fluid (dead oil, inhibited water).
2. **During shutdown:** Monitor cooldown time. Inject methanol or MEG before the deadline if restart is delayed.
3. **Restart sequence:** Follow a controlled startup procedure — ramp flow gradually, monitor pressure and temperature, and confirm inhibitor coverage before full-rate production.
4. **Emergency shutdowns:** These are the most dangerous because there is no time for pre-treatment. Cooldown time becomes the hard deadline for intervention.
